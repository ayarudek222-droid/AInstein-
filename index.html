A science desk that runs itself

AInstein

Keeping up with science is work: the good material is scattered across preprint servers, agency press rooms and paywalled journals, and none of it is shaped like something you'd read on a phone. This is an attempt to take that seriously in the other direction.

What it does

Every morning it pulls from arXiv, Crossref, NASA, ESA, CERN, magazines and YouTube, scores what's worth your attention, and rewrites the openly-licensed parts into plain language.

Two layers, per subject

A standing directory — the experiments, missions, labs and companies that exist, and where they are — and the pulse of what actually moved this week, attached to it.

The rule it obeys

Facts are free; someone else's sentences are not. Open sources get rewritten into cards. Journalism gets a headline and a link, and its text never reaches the AI at all.

A feed that ends

Today is a short set of cards, one at a time — news, a classic and something to watch — with a finish line. Mark what you learned, keep what you want, and the diary remembers it for you.
